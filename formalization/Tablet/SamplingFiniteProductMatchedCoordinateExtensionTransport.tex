\begin{helper}
Let two one-sided finite-coordinate extensions be computed from the same base
cell weight \(w_0\) under the same Bernoulli activation parameter \(p\).  On
the source side the extension factor is
\[
  p^{|A_D|}(1-p)^{|D|-|A_D|}
  \prod_v \begin{cases}t_v,&v\in K,\\1,&v\notin K,\end{cases}
\]
and on the target side the extension factor is the analogous expression with
primed finite data.  If the Bernoulli activation factors are equal and the
priority lower-cut products are equal, then the two normalized extension
weights are equal.
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
The source extension law expresses the source normalized extension weight as
\[
  w_0\cdot a\cdot q,
\]
where
\[
  a=p^{|A_D|}(1-p)^{|D|-|A_D|}
  \quad\text{and}\quad
  q=\prod_v \begin{cases}t_v,&v\in K,\\1,&v\notin K.\end{cases}
\]
The target extension law expresses the target normalized extension weight as
\[
  w_0\cdot a'\cdot q',
\]
with the corresponding primed activation factor \(a'\) and priority factor
\(q'\).  The hypotheses say exactly that \(a=a'\) and \(q=q'\).  Substituting
these two equalities into the two displayed formulas gives
\[
  w_0\cdot a\cdot q=w_0\cdot a'\cdot q',
\]
so the source and target normalized extension weights are equal.  This is the
pure finite-product algebraic transport step used after the actual source and
target one-sided extension formulas have been supplied by other registered
finite-product mass laws.
\end{proof}
