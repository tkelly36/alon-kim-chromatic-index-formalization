\begin{lemma}
Let $Q$ be a nibble completion datum with finite edge and color types,
and let $0<\gamma\le\Delta$. Put $T=\{(a,v):v\in\operatorname{Fin}(n_a)\}$
and $\nu=\operatorname{NibbleProductMeasure}_T(\gamma/\Delta)$.
Then $\nu$ is a probability measure. For each color $a$ there is a mass
function $\mu_a$ satisfying the random-independent-set sampling law for
$Q_a,\Delta,\gamma$, with
$\nu\{J_a=S\}=\operatorname{ofReal}(\mu_a(S))$ for every $S$.
The events $\{J_a=S\}$ and $\{e\in I_a\}$ are measurable, and every
$I_a(\omega)$ is independent in the line graph.
For arbitrary Borel sets $F_a\subseteq(\mathbb R^2)^{\operatorname{Fin}(n_a)}$,
\[
\nu\{\forall a,\ (\omega(a,v))_v\in F_a\}
=\prod_a\nu\{(\omega(a,v))_v\in F_a\}.
\]
For arbitrary Borel coordinate sets $s_t\subseteq\mathbb R^2$, moreover,
$\nu\{\forall t,\omega(t)\in s_t\}=\prod_t\kappa_{\gamma/\Delta}(s_t)$,
with $\kappa$ as in the product measure definition.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $p=\gamma/\Delta$. The hypotheses imply $\Delta>0$ and $0<p\le1$.
Each factor in \noderef{NibbleProductMeasure} has mass
$(p+1-p)\operatorname{Leb}([0,1])=1$. The defining rectangle formula for
a finite product measure proves the last assertion and total mass one.
Regroup the factors by color. This gives the displayed color formula first
for rectangles $F_a$ in the vertex coordinates. To extend it to arbitrary
Borel $F_a$, fix all but one $F_a$: the sets for which the equality holds
form a Dynkin system (both sides are finite measures), and contain the
rectangle pi-system generating the finite product Borel sigma algebra.
The pi-lambda theorem extends the equality in that argument. Repeating
for each of the finitely many colors proves the assertion.

For a fixed color, decode its activation set as the coordinates whose
first component equals $1$, and retain the second components as priorities.
This is measurable: each activation membership is a coordinate equality,
and all priority projections are measurable. Its push-forward has activation
atom mass $p^{|A|}(1-p)^{n_a-|A|}$ and joint lower-rectangle mass
$p^{|A|}(1-p)^{n_a-|A|}\prod_v t_v$ for $0\le t_v\le1$, by the coordinate
rectangle formula. By \noderef{SamplingRegularGraphSamplingLawExists},
choose a sampling law $\mu_a$ on the regular graph $Q_a$.
The underlying natural Borel measure supplied by
\noderef{RandomIndependentSetSampling} has exactly these activation and
lower-rectangle masses. Apply
\noderef{SamplingActivationRectangleMeasureUniqueness} to identify it
with the decoded color marginal; the atom masses are finite, being at most
one. Its push-forward clause therefore gives the asserted mass of $J_a=S$.

Every membership in \noderef{NibbleCompletedSelection} is a finite
intersection of coordinate equalities, complements, and strict priority
comparisons. All are Borel, so the finite selection fibers are measurable.
The definition in \noderef{NibbleSelectedEdges} then gives measurability
of original-edge selection. Two adjacent completed vertices cannot both
be selected: their two strict comparisons would give simultaneously
$q_u<q_v$ and $q_v<q_u$. Inducedness of the embeddings in
\noderef{NibbleCompletionData} transfers this independence to original
edges. This argument is pointwise, not merely almost sure.
\end{proof}
