\begin{helper}
Let \(G\) be a finite \(\Delta\)-regular graph and let \(u,v\) be distinct
nonadjacent vertices.  For real \(x,y\),
\[
\prod_{w\in N_G(u)\setminus N_G(v)}\left(1-\frac{x}{\Delta}\right)
\prod_{w\in N_G(v)}\left(1-\frac{y}{\Delta}\right)
=
\left(1-\frac{x}{\Delta}\right)^{\Delta-|N_G(u)\cap N_G(v)|}
\left(1-\frac{y}{\Delta}\right)^\Delta .
\]
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Because \(G\) is \(\Delta\)-regular, both \(N_G(u)\) and \(N_G(v)\) have
cardinality \(\Delta\).  The finite set \(N_G(u)\) is the disjoint union of
\(N_G(u)\setminus N_G(v)\) and \(N_G(u)\cap N_G(v)\), so
\[
|N_G(u)\setminus N_G(v)|=\Delta-|N_G(u)\cap N_G(v)|.
\]
The first product is a product of the same factor
\(1-x/\Delta\) over exactly this many vertices, and hence equals
\((1-x/\Delta)^{\Delta-|N_G(u)\cap N_G(v)|}\).  The second product is a product
of the same factor \(1-y/\Delta\) over all \(\Delta\) neighbors of \(v\), and
hence equals \((1-y/\Delta)^\Delta\).  Multiplying the two identities gives
the claimed formula.
\end{proof}
