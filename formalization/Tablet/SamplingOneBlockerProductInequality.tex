\begin{helper}
Let \(C\) be a finite nonempty candidate set, let \(x_0\in C\), and let
\(p\) be a real number with \(0\le p\le 1\).  Suppose every priority
\(\pi(x)\), \(x\in C\), lies in \([0,1]\).  Then
\[
\prod_{x\in C} p(1-\pi(x)) \le p(1-\pi(x_0)).
\]
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
For each \(x\in C\), the inequalities \(0\le p\) and \(\pi(x)\le 1\) give
\[
0\le p(1-\pi(x)).
\]
Also \(p\le 1\), \(1-\pi(x)\le 1\), and both factors are nonnegative, so
\[
p(1-\pi(x))\le 1.
\]
Since \(x_0\in C\), the singleton \(\{x_0\}\) is a subset of \(C\).  A product
of nonnegative real factors all at most \(1\) can only decrease when more
such factors are inserted.  Therefore
\[
\prod_{x\in C}p(1-\pi(x))
\le
\prod_{x\in \{x_0\}}p(1-\pi(x))
=p(1-\pi(x_0)),
\]
as claimed.
\end{proof}
