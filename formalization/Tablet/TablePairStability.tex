\begin{lemma}
Let $k,n\in\mathbb N$ with $k\ge2$, let $C>0$, and let
$A=(a_{i,j})$ be a nonnegative $k\times n$ real table whose column sums are at
most $C$.  Suppose there are distinct columns $j_1,j_2$ and a row $i^*$ such
that
\[
\left|
\sum_{i\ne i^*}(a_{i,j_1}+a_{i,j_2})
-2\left(1-\frac1k\right)C
\right|\ge \delta>0 .
\]
Then
\[
\sum_{j=1}^n\sum_{1\le i<i'\le k}a_{i,j}a_{i',j}
\le
\frac{k-1}{2k}nC^2-\frac{k\delta^2}{4(k-1)} .
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
This is the ``moreover'' part of the table-pair lemma from the paper.  We give
the argument since later 3-uniform 3-simple estimates use exactly this
stability loss.

For a selected column $j\in\{j_1,j_2\}$ put
\[
t_j=\sum_{i\ne i^*}a_{i,j}.
\]
The column-sum hypothesis gives $a_{i^*,j}\le C-t_j$.  By Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
\sum_{i\ne i^*}a_{i,j}^2\ge \frac{t_j^2}{k-1}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{i<i'}a_{i,j}a_{i',j}
&=a_{i^*,j}t_j+
\frac12\left(t_j^2-\sum_{i\ne i^*}a_{i,j}^2\right)\\
&\le (C-t_j)t_j+\frac{k-2}{2(k-1)}t_j^2\\
&=\frac{k-1}{2k}C^2
-\frac{k}{2(k-1)}
\left(t_j-\left(1-\frac1k\right)C\right)^2 .
\end{aligned}
\]
Now let $j\in[n]\setminus\{j_1,j_2\}$ be any unselected column, and put
\[
s_j=\sum_{i=1}^k a_{i,j}.
\]
Since the entries are nonnegative, $s_j\ge0$, and the column-sum hypothesis
gives $s_j\le C$.  For any real numbers $x_1,\ldots,x_k$ with sum $s$, the
finite identity
\[
\sum_{1\le i<i'\le k}x_ix_{i'}
=\frac{k-1}{2k}s^2
-\frac1{2k}\sum_{1\le i<i'\le k}(x_i-x_{i'})^2
\]
is obtained by expanding the right-hand side: the square-sum terms cancel to
leave exactly the cross-products.  Applying this identity to the column
$x_i=a_{i,j}$ and using that every square is nonnegative gives
\[
\sum_{i<i'}a_{i,j}a_{i',j}
\le \frac{k-1}{2k}s_j^2 .
\]
Because $k\ge2$, the coefficient $(k-1)/(2k)$ is nonnegative.  Also
$s_j\ge0$ and $s_j\le C$, so multiplying the inequality $s_j\le C$ by the
nonnegative number $s_j$ gives $s_j^2\le Cs_j$.  Since the column-sum
hypothesis also has $C>0$ and $s_j\le C$, multiplying $s_j\le C$ by the
nonnegative number $C$ gives $Cs_j\le C^2$.  Therefore
\[
s_j^2\le C^2,
\]
and multiplying by the nonnegative coefficient $(k-1)/(2k)$ yields
\[
\sum_{i<i'}a_{i,j}a_{i',j}\le \frac{k-1}{2k}C^2 .
\]
Let
\[
x=t_{j_1}-\left(1-\frac1k\right)C,\qquad
y=t_{j_2}-\left(1-\frac1k\right)C .
\]
The hypothesis says $|x+y|\ge\delta$, so
$x^2+y^2\ge (x+y)^2/2\ge\delta^2/2$.  Summing the column bounds over all
columns yields
\[
\sum_j\sum_{i<i'}a_{i,j}a_{i',j}
\le \frac{k-1}{2k}nC^2
-\frac{k}{2(k-1)}(x^2+y^2)
\le \frac{k-1}{2k}nC^2-\frac{k\delta^2}{4(k-1)} .
\]
\end{proof}
