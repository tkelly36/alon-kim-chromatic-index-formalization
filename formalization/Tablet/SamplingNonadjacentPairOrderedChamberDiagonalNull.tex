\begin{helper}
Let \(V\) be finite and let \(\nu\) be a measure on activation--priority
pairs \((A,\pi)\in\mathcal P(V)\times\mathbb R^V\).  Fix
\(0\le p\le1\).  Assume that every activation atom satisfies
\[
\nu\{A=A_0\}=
\operatorname{ofReal}\!\left(p^{|A_0|}(1-p)^{|V|-|A_0|}\right)
\]
for every \(A_0\subseteq V\).  Assume also that activated priorities are
supported in the unit cube on each atom, in the sense that
\[
\nu\{A=A_0,\ \pi(q)\in[0,1]\text{ for every }q\in A_0\}
=\nu\{A=A_0\}
\]
for every \(A_0\subseteq V\).  Finally, assume the full lower-rectangle
product law: for every \(A_0\subseteq V\) and every threshold vector
\(t:V\to\mathbb R\) with \(t(q)\in[0,1]\) for all \(q\),
\[
\nu\{A=A_0,\ 0\le \pi(q)\le t(q)\text{ for every }q\in V\}
=\nu\{A=A_0\}\operatorname{ofReal}\!\left(\prod_{q\in V}t(q)\right).
\]
If \(u\ne v\), then
\[
\nu\{u,v\in A,\ \pi(q)\in[0,1]\text{ for every }q\in A,\ \pi(u)=\pi(v)\}=0 .
\]
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
For an activation atom \(A_0\), write
\[
\alpha(A_0)=p^{|A_0|}(1-p)^{|V|-|A_0|}.
\]
The activation-atom hypothesis gives
\(\nu\{A=A_0\}=\operatorname{ofReal}(\alpha(A_0))\), and \(0\le p\le1\)
implies \(\alpha(A_0)\ge0\).  Let
\[
Q=\{(A,\pi): A=A_0,\ 0\le \pi(q)\le1\hbox{ for every }q\in V\}.
\]
Applying the lower-rectangle hypothesis with the constant threshold vector
\(t(q)=1\) gives
\[
\nu(Q)=\nu\{A=A_0\}\operatorname{ofReal}\!\left(\prod_{q\in V}1\right)
=\nu\{A=A_0\}.
\]
Thus the complement of \(Q\) inside the atom \(A=A_0\) has \(\nu\)-measure
\(0\).

Now restrict attention to this full cube.  For every threshold vector
\(t:V\to[0,1]\), the lower-rectangle hypothesis says that the atom-restricted
measure of the anchored rectangle
\(\prod_{q\in V}[0,t(q)]\) is
\[
\operatorname{ofReal}(\alpha(A_0))\,
\operatorname{ofReal}\!\left(\prod_{q\in V}t(q)\right).
\]
Equivalently, on every anchored rectangle in \([0,1]^V\), the push-forward of
\(\nu\) from the atom to the priority coordinates agrees with the finite
measure
\[
\operatorname{ofReal}(\alpha(A_0))\cdot\lambda_V,
\]
where \(\lambda_V\) is product Lebesgue measure on the finite cube
\([0,1]^V\).  The anchored rectangles are closed under finite intersections
and generate the Borel sigma-algebra of the cube.  Both finite measures have
the same total mass, namely \(\operatorname{ofReal}(\alpha(A_0))\), by the
constant-threshold-\(1\) calculation above.  Hence the finite-measure
uniqueness theorem identifies the atom-restricted priority law on all Borel
subsets of the full cube with
\(\operatorname{ofReal}(\alpha(A_0))\lambda_V\).

Assume first that \(u,v\in A_0\).  Since \(u\ne v\), the diagonal
\[
D=\{\pi\in[0,1]^V:\pi(u)=\pi(v)\}
\]
is a coordinate hyperplane in the finite product cube.  By Fubini over the
\(u\)-coordinate, after fixing all coordinates except \(u\), the section of
\(D\) is the singleton \(\{\pi(v)\}\).  A singleton has one-dimensional
Lebesgue measure \(0\); this is the fixed-coordinate zero-length section that
one obtains from the interval-mass calculation of
\noderef{SamplingFinitePriorityOneCoordinateIntervalMass} after passing to
the one-dimensional section.  Therefore \(\lambda_V(D)=0\), and the
atomwise diagonal part
\[
\{A=A_0,\ 0\le\pi(q)\le1\hbox{ for every }q\in V,\ \pi(u)=\pi(v)\}
\]
has \(\nu\)-measure
\(\operatorname{ofReal}(\alpha(A_0))\lambda_V(D)=0\).  If \(u\notin A_0\) or
\(v\notin A_0\), then the atomwise part of the displayed event is empty,
because the displayed event requires \(u,v\in A\).

It remains only to pass from the full-cube atomwise statement to the event in
the theorem, which asks for cube bounds only on activated coordinates.  On
the atom \(A=A_0\), the constant-threshold-\(1\) lower-rectangle calculation
above says
\[
\nu\{A=A_0,\ 0\le \pi(q)\le1\hbox{ for every }q\in V\}
=\nu\{A=A_0\}.
\]
Thus, inside this atom, the complement of the full cube
\[
\{0\le \pi(q)\le1\hbox{ for every }q\in V\}
\]
has measure \(0\).  Now take a point in the atomwise part of the theorem's
diagonal event.  If this point lies in the full cube, then it lies in the
full-cube diagonal part already proved null.  If it does not lie in the full
cube, then it lies in the zero-measure complement just identified.  Therefore
the atomwise part of the theorem's diagonal event is contained in the union of
the full-cube diagonal part and the complement of the full cube inside the
atom.  Both pieces have \(\nu\)-measure \(0\), so every activation atom
contributes measure \(0\).  There are finitely many atoms because \(V\) is
finite, and the displayed diagonal event is the finite union of these atomwise
parts.  Finite subadditivity gives total \(\nu\)-measure \(0\).
\end{proof}
