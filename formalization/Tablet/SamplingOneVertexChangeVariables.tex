\begin{helper}
Let $\Delta$ be a positive integer and let $\gamma$ be real.  Then the
substitution $x=\gamma(1-a)$ gives
\[
\frac{\gamma}{\Delta}\int_0^1
\left(1-\frac{\gamma}{\Delta}(1-a)\right)^\Delta\,da
=
\frac1\Delta\int_0^\gamma
\left(1-\frac{x}{\Delta}\right)^\Delta\,dx .
\]
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Let
\[
f(x)=\left(1-\frac{x}{\Delta}\right)^\Delta .
\]
If $\gamma=0$, both sides are zero: the left side has the factor $\gamma$,
and the right side is an integral over the degenerate interval $[0,0]$.

Assume now that $\gamma\ne0$.  The affine substitution formula for interval
integrals gives, for the map $a\mapsto \gamma-\gamma a$,
\[
\int_0^1 f(\gamma-\gamma a)\,da
=
\gamma^{-1}\int_{\gamma-\gamma\cdot 1}^{\gamma-\gamma\cdot 0} f(x)\,dx
=
\gamma^{-1}\int_0^\gamma f(x)\,dx .
\]
For every $a$,
\[
f(\gamma-\gamma a)
=\left(1-\frac{\gamma-\gamma a}{\Delta}\right)^\Delta
=\left(1-\frac{\gamma}{\Delta}(1-a)\right)^\Delta .
\]
Substituting this identity into the preceding display and multiplying by
$\gamma/\Delta$ gives
\[
\frac{\gamma}{\Delta}\int_0^1
\left(1-\frac{\gamma}{\Delta}(1-a)\right)^\Delta\,da
=
\frac{\gamma}{\Delta}\gamma^{-1}\int_0^\gamma f(x)\,dx
=
\frac1\Delta\int_0^\gamma
\left(1-\frac{x}{\Delta}\right)^\Delta\,dx,
\]
as required.
\end{proof}
