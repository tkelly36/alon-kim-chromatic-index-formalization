\begin{helper}
Let $k$ be a natural number and let $w_{ij}$ be a real weight assigned to
each ordered pair of indices in $\{1,\ldots,k\}$.  Then the symmetric
incident sum
\[
\sum_i\sum_j
\begin{cases}
w_{ij},& i<j,\\
w_{ji},& j<i,\\
0,& i=j
\end{cases}
\]
is equal to twice the increasing-pair sum:
\[
\sum_i\sum_j
\begin{cases}
w_{ij},& i<j,\\
w_{ji},& j<i,\\
0,& i=j
\end{cases}
=
2\sum_{i<j}w_{ij}.
\]
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Split the displayed double sum into the contribution of the ordered pairs
with $i<j$ and the contribution of the ordered pairs with $j<i$; the diagonal
terms contribute $0$.  The first contribution is exactly
$\sum_{i<j}w_{ij}$.  In the second contribution, rename the ordered pair
$(i,j)$ as $(j,i)$; this is a bijection from the ordered pairs with $j<i$ to
the ordered pairs with $i<j$, and under this renaming the summand $w_{ji}$
becomes $w_{ij}$.  Hence the second contribution is also
$\sum_{i<j}w_{ij}$.  Adding the two equal contributions gives
$2\sum_{i<j}w_{ij}$.
\end{proof}
