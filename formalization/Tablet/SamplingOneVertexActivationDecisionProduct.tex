\begin{helper}
Fix a finite graph $G$, a vertex $v$, real numbers $p,q$, and a priority
threshold $a$.  Sum over all activation-decision functions
$b:V(G)\to\{\mathrm{inactive},\mathrm{active}\}$.  Give an activated copy of
$v$ weight $p$, an inactive copy of $v$ weight $0$, an activated neighbor of
$v$ weight $pa$, an inactive neighbor of $v$ weight $q$, an activated
non-neighbor different from $v$ weight $p$, and an inactive non-neighbor
different from $v$ weight $q$.  Then
\[
\sum_b\prod_{u\in V(G)} W_u(b(u))
=
p\prod_{u\in V(G)}
\begin{cases}
1, & u=v,\\
q+pa, & u\sim_G v,\\
q+p, & u\ne v\text{ and }u\not\sim_G v.
\end{cases}
\]
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Expand the product of the two possible local weights at every vertex:
\[
\prod_{u\in V(G)}\bigl(W_u(\mathrm{active})+
W_u(\mathrm{inactive})\bigr)
=\sum_b\prod_{u\in V(G)}W_u(b(u)).
\]
This is just the finite distributive law, choosing one of the two summands at
each vertex.

Now compute the local sums.  At $u=v$ the inactive weight is $0$ and the
active weight is $p$, so the factor is $p$.  At a neighbor $u$ of $v$, with
$u\ne v$ because the graph is simple, the active weight is $pa$ and the
inactive weight is $q$, giving $q+pa$.  At every remaining vertex different
from $v$, the two weights are $p$ and $q$, giving $q+p$.  Pulling out the
special factor $p$ at $v$ and replacing that vertex's factor in the product by
$1$ gives exactly the displayed formula.
\end{proof}
