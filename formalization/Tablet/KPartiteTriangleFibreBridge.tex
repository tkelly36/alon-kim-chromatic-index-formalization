\begin{helper}
Let $G$ be a finite $k$-partite graph with part map
$\operatorname{part}:V(G)\to [k]$.  Let $T$ be the set of three-element
cliques of $G$.  For each increasing triple $i<j<\ell$ of part labels, let
$A_i,A_j,A_\ell$ be the corresponding fibres of the part map, and let
$N_{ij\ell}$ be the number of ordered triples
$(a,b,c)\in A_i\times A_j\times A_\ell$ such that $ab,ac,bc$ are all
edges of $G$.  Then
\[
|T|\le \sum_{i<j<\ell} N_{ij\ell}.
\]
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
For every increasing triple $i<j<\ell$ and every ordered triangle
$(a,b,c)\in A_i\times A_j\times A_\ell$, the three labels are distinct.
Hence $a,b,c$ are distinct vertices, and the unordered set
$\{a,b,c\}$ is a three-element clique of $G$.  Forgetting the order
therefore defines a map from the disjoint union of all ordered fibre
triangle sets, indexed by $i<j<\ell$, onto a subset of $T$.

We claim this map is in fact onto all of $T$.  Let $S\in T$.  By definition
$S$ has three vertices and every two distinct vertices in $S$ are adjacent.
By \noderef{KPartiteTriangleDistinctParts}, the three part labels appearing
on $S$ are also three distinct labels.  Put these labels in increasing order
$i<j<\ell$.  For each of the labels $i,j,\ell$, choose the unique vertex of
$S$ with that label.  Uniqueness follows because two distinct vertices of
$S$ are adjacent, while the partite hypothesis forbids adjacent vertices
from having the same part label.  Call the chosen vertices $a,b,c$ in the
order of the labels $i,j,\ell$.  Then $(a,b,c)$ lies in
$A_i\times A_j\times A_\ell$, and since $S$ is a clique the three graph
edges $ab,ac,bc$ are present.  Thus $(a,b,c)$ is counted by
$N_{ij\ell}$, and forgetting its order gives exactly $S$.

Consequently the disjoint union of the ordered fibre triangle sets
surjects onto $T$.  A finite set cannot have larger cardinality than a
finite set that surjects onto it, so $|T|$ is at most the cardinality of
that disjoint union.  The latter cardinality is precisely
$\sum_{i<j<\ell}N_{ij\ell}$, proving the desired inequality.
\end{proof}
