\begin{helper}
Let \(V\) be finite, let \(u\ne v\), and let \(B_U,B_T\subseteq
V\setminus\{u,v\}\) be disjoint.  The volume of the set of all priority
vectors \(q\in\mathbb R^V\) such that every coordinate lies in \([0,1]\),
\(q(v)\le q(u)\), \(q(z)<q(u)\) for every \(z\in B_U\), and
\(q(z)<q(v)\) for every \(z\in B_T\), is
\[
\operatorname{ofReal}\left(\int_0^1\int_0^a
a^{|B_U|}b^{|B_T|}\,db\,da\right).
\]
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Split the two distinguished coordinates from the complementary coordinates
by the map
\[
q\mapsto\bigl(q(u),q(v),(q(w))_{w\ne u,v}\bigr).
\]
By \(\noderef{SamplingTwoCoordinateComplementMeasurePreserving}\), this map
preserves Lebesgue product measure.  Therefore the required volume is the
volume, in
\(\mathbb R\times\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^{V\setminus\{u,v\}}\), of the set
of triples \((a,b,r)\) with \(0\le a\le1\), \(0\le b\le a\), all
complementary coordinates \(r(w)\in[0,1]\), all \(B_U\)-coordinates
\(<a\), and all \(B_T\)-coordinates \(<b\).

This split set is Borel measurable, since it is described by finitely many
closed interval conditions and strict coordinate inequalities.  Applying
Tonelli's product-section formula first in the \(a\)-coordinate and then in
the \(b\)-coordinate writes its volume as the iterated nonnegative integral
over \((a,b)\) of the volume of the complementary-coordinate fiber.  If
\((a,b)\) is outside the simplex \(0\le b\le a\le1\), that fiber is empty.
If \(0\le b\le a\le1\), then
\(\noderef{SamplingTwoCoordinateChamberFiberVolume}\) gives the fiber volume
exactly as \(a^{|B_U|}b^{|B_T|}\).

Thus the volume is
\[
\int_{\mathbb R}^{+}\int_{\mathbb R}^{+}
\mathbf 1_{\{0\le b\le a\le1\}}\,
\operatorname{ofReal}(a^{|B_U|}b^{|B_T|})\,db\,da .
\]
The integrand \(a^{|B_U|}b^{|B_T|}\) is continuous.  On the simplex it is
nonnegative, and replacing the closed intervals by half-open intervals does
not change Lebesgue measure.  The standard conversion between the
nonnegative integral of \(\operatorname{ofReal}\) over an oriented interval
and \(\operatorname{ofReal}\) of the real interval integral, applied first in
\(b\) over \((0,a]\) and then in \(a\) over \((0,1]\), gives precisely
\[
\operatorname{ofReal}\left(\int_0^1\int_0^a
a^{|B_U|}b^{|B_T|}\,db\,da\right).
\]
Combining the measure-preserving coordinate split, the two applications of
Tonelli, the cited fiber-volume identity, and this interval-integral
conversion proves the claimed formula.
\end{proof}
