\begin{helper}
Fix a measure \(\nu\), a finite linearly ordered index type, witness events
\((W_i)_i\), a set \(U\), and a cell \(C\).  Assume
\[
  U=\bigcup_i W_i,
\]
assume that every witness event \(W_i\) is null-measurable with respect to
\(\nu\), and assume that \(C\) is null-measurable with respect to \(\nu\).
Then there are first-witness pieces \((P_i)_i\) such that, for every \(i\),
\[
  P_i=\{a:a\in W_i\hbox{ and }a\notin W_j\hbox{ for every }j<i\},
\]
such that the cell-restricted union identity
\[
  U\cap C=\bigcup_i(P_i\cap C)
\]
holds, such that the family \((P_i\cap C)_i\) is pairwise almost-disjoint
with respect to \(\nu\), and such that every restricted first-witness piece
\(P_i\cap C\) is null-measurable with respect to \(\nu\).
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Under the stated hypotheses, apply
\(\noderef{SamplingSplitOutsideBlockerFirstWitnessFiniteRefinement}\) to the
finite union \(U=\bigcup_i W_i\).  It supplies pieces
\[
  P_i=\{a:a\in W_i\hbox{ and }a\notin W_j\hbox{ for all }j<i\},
\]
proves \(U=\bigcup_iP_i\), and proves that the family \(P_i\) is pairwise
disjoint.

Intersect the equality \(U=\bigcup_iP_i\) with the fixed cell \(C\).  Set
intersection distributes over arbitrary unions, so
\[
  U\cap C=(\bigcup_iP_i)\cap C=\bigcup_i(P_i\cap C).
\]
If \(i\ne j\), then \(P_i\cap P_j=\emptyset\) by the cited refinement node.
Hence
\[
  (P_i\cap C)\cap(P_j\cap C)\subseteq P_i\cap P_j=\emptyset.
\]
Thus the restricted pieces are pairwise disjoint.  A pairwise disjoint
family is pairwise almost-disjoint for any measure, because every
intersection of two distinct pieces is empty and therefore has measure zero.

It remains only to check null-measurability.  For a fixed \(i\), the piece
\(P_i\) is the intersection of \(W_i\) with the complements of the finitely
many earlier witness events \(W_j\), \(j<i\).  Since each \(W_j\) is
null-measurable, complements of these \(W_j\)'s are null-measurable, and a
finite intersection of null-measurable sets is null-measurable.  Therefore
\(P_i\) is null-measurable.  Intersecting with the null-measurable cell
\(C\) preserves null-measurability, so \(P_i\cap C\) is null-measurable for
every \(i\).
\end{proof}
