\begin{helper}
Let \(0<\gamma\le \Delta\), let \(c\le\Delta\), and let
\(0\le x\le y\le \gamma\).  Then the ordered-chamber integrand appearing in
the nonadjacent-pair calculation is nonnegative:
\[
0\le
\left(1-\frac{x}{\Delta}\right)^{\Delta-c}
\cdot
\left(1-\frac{y}{\Delta}\right)^\Delta .
\]
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Since \(0<\gamma\le \Delta\), the real number \(\Delta\) is positive.  From
\(x\le y\le\gamma\le\Delta\) we have \(x/\Delta\le1\), and therefore
\(1-x/\Delta\ge0\).  Likewise \(y\le\gamma\le\Delta\) gives
\(y/\Delta\le1\), and hence \(1-y/\Delta\ge0\).  Nonnegative real numbers
remain nonnegative after raising to a natural-number power, so both
\((1-x/\Delta)^{\Delta-c}\) and \((1-y/\Delta)^\Delta\) are nonnegative.
Their product is therefore nonnegative.
\end{proof}
