\begin{helper}
Let $G$ be a finite graph, let $X\subseteq V(G)$, and let $\mu$ be a real
weight on subsets of $V(G)$.  Assume every set $S$ with $\mu(S)\ne0$ is
independent in $G$.  Let $\mathcal J_3$ be the family of three-element
independent subsets of $X$.  Then
\[
\sum_S \mu(S)\binom{|S\cap X|}{3}
=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal J_3}\sum_{S:Q\subseteq S}\mu(S).
\]
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
For a fixed subset $S$, the number $\binom{|S\cap X|}{3}$ is exactly the
number of three-element subsets $Q$ of $S\cap X$.  Thus
\[
\binom{|S\cap X|}{3}
=\sum_Q {\bf 1}_{\{|Q|=3,\ Q\subseteq X,\ Q\subseteq S\}}.
\]
If $\mu(S)=0$, multiplying by $\mu(S)$ makes the contribution of this $S$
zero.  If $\mu(S)\ne0$, the hypothesis says that $S$ is independent in $G$;
hence every subset $Q\subseteq S$ is independent.  Therefore, on the support
of $\mu$, the displayed indicator is the same as
${\bf 1}_{\{Q\in\mathcal J_3,\ Q\subseteq S\}}$.  Multiplying by $\mu(S)$ and
summing over all $S$ gives
\[
\sum_S\mu(S)\binom{|S\cap X|}{3}
=\sum_S\sum_Q \mu(S){\bf 1}_{\{Q\in\mathcal J_3,\ Q\subseteq S\}}.
\]
The vertex set is finite, so both sums are finite and may be interchanged.
After interchanging them, the terms with $Q\notin\mathcal J_3$ vanish, and
the remaining inner sum is precisely $\sum_{S:Q\subseteq S}\mu(S)$.

\end{proof}
