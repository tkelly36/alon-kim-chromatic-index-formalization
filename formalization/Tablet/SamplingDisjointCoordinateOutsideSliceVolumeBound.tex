\begin{helper}
Let \(V\) be finite.  Let \(a,b,c\in V\) be pairwise distinct, and let
\(S_x,S_y,S_z\subseteq V\) be pairwise disjoint and disjoint from
\(\{a,b,c\}\).  If \(0\le x\le y\le z\le1\), then the product Lebesgue
measure, on the coordinates \(V\setminus\{a,b,c\}\), of the slice of the
unit-cube chamber with \(q_a=x\), \(q_b=y\), and \(q_c=z\) is at most
\[
\operatorname{ofReal}\bigl(x^{|S_x|}y^{|S_y|}z^{|S_z|}\bigr).
\]
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(0\le x\le y\le z\le1\).  In the slice with distinguished coordinates
\(q_a=x\), \(q_b=y\), and \(q_c=z\), each coordinate in \(S_x\) must lie in
\([0,x)\), each coordinate in \(S_y\) must lie in \([0,y)\), and each
coordinate in \(S_z\) must lie in \([0,z)\).  Every other outside coordinate
is only constrained to lie in \([0,1]\).  The hypotheses that
\(S_x,S_y,S_z\) are pairwise disjoint and avoid \(\{a,b,c\}\) make these
coordinate restrictions independent and ensure that no outside coordinate is
assigned two different thresholds.

The slice is therefore contained in the product box whose side lengths are
\(x\) on the \(S_x\)-coordinates, \(y\) on the \(S_y\)-coordinates, \(z\) on
the \(S_z\)-coordinates, and \(1\) on all remaining outside coordinates.
Finite product Lebesgue measure of this box is the product of its side
lengths.  Since \(x,y,z\) are nonnegative, this product is
\(x^{|S_x|}y^{|S_y|}z^{|S_z|}\), and monotonicity of measure gives the stated
upper bound.  The strict inequalities only shrink the corresponding intervals,
so no boundary correction is needed for this upper-bound form.
\end{proof}
