\begin{lemma}
Let $V,W$ be finite sets, $\phi:V\to W$ injective, and $S\subseteq V$.
There exist a finite tag set $T$ and injections $s:V\to T$, $t:W\to T$
such that $s(v)=t(w)$ if and only if $v\in S$ and $\phi(v)=w$.
Every tag belongs to the image of $s$ or of $t$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Take $T=V\sqcup(W\setminus\phi(S))$ and $s(v)=\operatorname{inl}(v)$.
For $w\in\phi(S)$, let $t(w)=\operatorname{inl}(v)$ for its unique
preimage $v\in S$; existence follows from image membership and uniqueness
from injectivity. Otherwise put $t(w)=\operatorname{inr}(w)$.
The two summands are disjoint, so equality of a source and target tag is
exactly the stated condition. The source map is injective. Two equal target
tags are either both right tags, when their labels coincide, or both left
tags, when applying $\phi$ to their equal labels gives equal vertices.
Each left tag is a source tag and each right tag is its labelled target's
tag. This construction also applies when either finite set is empty.
\end{proof}
