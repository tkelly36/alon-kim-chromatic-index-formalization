\begin{helper}
Let \(V\) be finite.  Let \(a,b,c\in V\) be distinct, and let
\(S_x,S_y,S_z\subseteq V\) be pairwise disjoint and disjoint from
\(\{a,b,c\}\).  The Lebesgue product measure of the unit-cube chamber
\[
C=\{q:q_a\le q_b\le q_c,\ q_w<q_a\ (w\in S_x),\
q_w<q_b\ (w\in S_y),\ q_w<q_c\ (w\in S_z),\
q_v\in[0,1]\ (v\in V)\}
\]
is at most
\[
\operatorname{ofReal}\left(\int_0^1\int_0^z\int_0^y
x^{|S_x|}y^{|S_y|}z^{|S_z|}\,dx\,dy\,dz\right).
\]
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Use Fubini for product Lebesgue measure on the finite product
\(\mathbb R^V\), ordering the integrations by the three distinguished
coordinates \(q_c=z\), \(q_b=y\), and \(q_a=x\).  The chamber condition on
these three coordinates is exactly \(0\le x\le y\le z\le1\).

For fixed \(x,y,z\) in this simplex, the remaining coordinates separate
because the sets \(S_x,S_y,S_z\) are pairwise disjoint and avoid
\(\{a,b,c\}\).  A coordinate in \(S_x\) is allowed to range over \([0,x)\),
contributing length \(x\); a coordinate in \(S_y\) contributes length \(y\);
and a coordinate in \(S_z\) contributes length \(z\).  Every remaining
coordinate ranges over the whole interval \([0,1]\) and contributes length
\(1\).  Thus the conditional product volume of the outside coordinates is
\(x^{|S_x|}y^{|S_y|}z^{|S_z|}\).

The strict outside inequalities differ from weak interval constraints only on
finitely many coordinate hyperplanes.  Each such hyperplane has product
Lebesgue measure zero, and a finite union of null hyperplanes is null.  Hence
the chamber volume is exactly the displayed iterated integral.  The Lean
statement asks only for the corresponding upper bound, so this equality
immediately implies the claim.
\end{proof}
