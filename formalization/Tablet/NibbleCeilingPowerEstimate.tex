\begin{lemma}
Let $\iota,\gamma,\Delta$ be real numbers with $\gamma\ge1$, $\Delta\ge4$,
$e^{-\gamma}\le\min(1/4,\iota/8)$, $1/\gamma\le\iota/4$, and
$2/\Delta\le\iota/8$. Then
\[
\left(1-\frac{1-e^{-\gamma}}\Delta+\frac2{\Delta^2}\right)^{\lceil\Delta/\gamma\rceil}
\le 1-\frac{1-\iota/2}{\gamma}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $u=1-e^{-\gamma}-2/\Delta$. Since $2/\Delta\le1/2$ and
$e^{-\gamma}\le1/4$, we have $0\le u\le1$. In particular
$0\le1-u/\Delta$. The inequality $1+x\le e^x$, followed by
$\lceil\Delta/\gamma\rceil\ge\Delta/\gamma$, gives
\[
(1-u/\Delta)^{\lceil\Delta/\gamma\rceil}
\le e^{-u\lceil\Delta/\gamma\rceil/\Delta}\le e^{-u/\gamma}.
\]
Since $0\le u/\gamma\le1$, the exponential remainder estimate
$|e^x-1-x|\le x^2$ for $|x|\le1$ bounds the last expression by
$1-u/\gamma+u^2/\gamma^2$. Moreover $u^2\le1$, so
\[
e^{-\gamma}+2/\Delta+u^2/\gamma
\le \iota/8+\iota/8+\iota/4=\iota/2.
\]
Dividing this inequality by $\gamma>0$ and substituting the definition
of $u$ proves the claimed bound.
\end{proof}
