\begin{helper}
Let $G$ be a finite graph with a part map
$\operatorname{part}:V(G)\to [k]$.  For each increasing triple
$i<j<\ell$ of parts, let $A_i,A_j,A_\ell$ be the corresponding fibres of
the part map, and let $m_{ij},m_{i\ell},m_{j\ell}$ be the ordered edge
counts between the corresponding pairs of fibres.  Then the sum, over all
increasing triples of parts, of the number of ordered triangles with one
vertex in each of the three fibres is at most
\[
\sum_{i<j<\ell}\sqrt{m_{ij}m_{i\ell}m_{j\ell}}.
\]
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Fix one increasing triple $i<j<\ell$.  Apply
\noderef{KPartiteFixedThreePartProductEstimate} to the three finite vertex
sets $A_i,A_j,A_\ell$.  Its statement says exactly that the number of ordered
triples $(a,b,c)\in A_i\times A_j\times A_\ell$ spanning all three graph
edges is at most the square root of the product of the three ordered
pair-edge counts between $A_i,A_j$, between $A_i,A_\ell$, and between
$A_j,A_\ell$.

This pointwise inequality holds for every increasing triple of parts.  The
indexing set of such triples is finite, and summing a pointwise inequality
over a finite set preserves the inequality.  The three pair-edge counts in
the cited node are precisely the numbers denoted
$m_{ij},m_{i\ell},m_{j\ell}$ here, because each fibre is defined by the
corresponding value of the part map.  Summing the pointwise bounds over all
$i<j<\ell$ gives the displayed inequality.
\end{proof}
