\begin{helper}
Let $k$ be a natural number and let $w_{ab}$ be a real weight assigned to
each ordered pair of indices in $\{1,\ldots,k\}$.  Define the associated
symmetric unordered-pair weight by
\[
e(a,b)=
\begin{cases}
w_{ab},&a<b,\\
w_{ba},&b<a,\\
0,&a=b.
\end{cases}
\]
For each vertex $i$, let
\[
T_i=\sum_{\substack{j<\ell\\j,\ell\ne i}}
\sqrt{e(i,j)e(i,\ell)e(j,\ell)}.
\]
Then the vertex-through triangle sums double count the increasing weighted
triangle sum exactly three times:
\[
\sum_i T_i
=3\sum_{a<b<c}\sqrt{w_{ab}w_{ac}w_{bc}}.
\]
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Rewrite the left hand side as one finite sum over triples $(i,j,\ell)$ with
$j<\ell$, $i\ne j$, and $i\ne \ell$:
\[
\sum_i T_i
=
\sum_{\substack{j<\ell\\ i\ne j,\ i\ne \ell}}
\sqrt{e(i,j)e(i,\ell)e(j,\ell)}.
\]
This is just the bijection from a vertex $i$ and an ordered pair
$(j,\ell)$ in the complement of $i$ with $j<\ell$ to the flat triple
$(i,j,\ell)$ satisfying those three displayed conditions.

Because $j<\ell$ and $i$ is distinct from both $j$ and $\ell$, each flat
triple lies in exactly one of the three cases
\[
i<j<\ell,\qquad j<i<\ell,\qquad j<\ell<i.
\]
These three cases are disjoint and exhaustive.  In the first case the flat
triple is already the increasing triple $(i,j,\ell)$, and the summand is
\[
\sqrt{w_{ij}w_{i\ell}w_{j\ell}}.
\]
In the second case, the map $(i,j,\ell)\mapsto (j,i,\ell)$ is a bijection
from triples with $j<i<\ell$ to increasing triples.  Since the symmetric
weight satisfies $e(i,j)=w_{ji}$, $e(i,\ell)=w_{i\ell}$, and
$e(j,\ell)=w_{j\ell}$, the summand becomes
\[
\sqrt{w_{ji}w_{i\ell}w_{j\ell}},
\]
which is the increasing-triple summand for $(j,i,\ell)$, up to the
commutativity of multiplication.  In the third case, the map
$(i,j,\ell)\mapsto (j,\ell,i)$ is a bijection from triples with
$j<\ell<i$ to increasing triples.  There
$e(i,j)=w_{ji}$, $e(i,\ell)=w_{\ell i}$, and $e(j,\ell)=w_{j\ell}$, so the
summand is the increasing-triple summand for $(j,\ell,i)$, again up to
commutativity of multiplication.

Thus each of the three disjoint pieces is exactly
\[
\sum_{a<b<c}\sqrt{w_{ab}w_{ac}w_{bc}},
\]
and adding the three equal contributions gives the claimed identity.
\end{proof}
