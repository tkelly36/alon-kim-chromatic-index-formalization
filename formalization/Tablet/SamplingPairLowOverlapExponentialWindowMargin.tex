\begin{helper}
Fix \(0<\alpha\le1\), and let \(\gamma_0\ge0\).  Suppose that the
exponential tail of this window satisfies
\[
e^{-\gamma_0}\left(1+\frac1\alpha\right)\le \alpha .
\]
Then, uniformly for every \(\lambda\in[\alpha,1]\),
\[
2\int_0^{\gamma_0}\int_x^{\gamma_0}
(1-\alpha)e^{-\lambda x}(1-\alpha)e^{-y}\,dy\,dx
\ge
\frac{2}{1+\lambda}-6\alpha .
\]
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(\lambda\in[\alpha,1]\).  Since \(0<\alpha\le\lambda\), we have
\(\lambda>0\) and \(1+\lambda>0\).  First compute the one-dimensional
exponential primitive: for every \(c>0\),
\[
\int_a^b e^{-cy}\,dy=\frac{e^{-ca}-e^{-cb}}{c}.
\]
This is the fundamental theorem of calculus applied to
\(-e^{-ct}/c\), whose derivative is \(e^{-ct}\).

For each fixed \(x\), applying this formula with \(c=1\) gives
\[
\int_x^{\gamma_0}
(1-\alpha)e^{-\lambda x}(1-\alpha)e^{-y}\,dy
=
(1-\alpha)^2e^{-\lambda x}\bigl(e^{-x}-e^{-\gamma_0}\bigr).
\]
Therefore the left-hand side is
\[
2(1-\alpha)^2 J,
\quad
J:=\int_0^{\gamma_0}
e^{-\lambda x}\bigl(e^{-x}-e^{-\gamma_0}\bigr)\,dx .
\]
Using the same primitive with \(c=1+\lambda\) and \(c=\lambda\),
\[
J=
\frac{1-e^{-(1+\lambda)\gamma_0}}{1+\lambda}
-e^{-\gamma_0}\frac{1-e^{-\lambda\gamma_0}}{\lambda}.
\]

We now bound the two omitted tails uniformly in \(\lambda\).  Since
\(\gamma_0\ge0\) and \(\lambda>0\),
\[
\frac{e^{-(1+\lambda)\gamma_0}}{1+\lambda}\le e^{-\gamma_0}.
\]
Also \(1-e^{-\lambda\gamma_0}\le1\) and \(\lambda\ge\alpha>0\), so
\[
e^{-\gamma_0}\frac{1-e^{-\lambda\gamma_0}}{\lambda}
\le e^{-\gamma_0}\frac1\alpha .
\]
The assumed tail condition gives
\[
J\ge
\frac1{1+\lambda}
-e^{-\gamma_0}\left(1+\frac1\alpha\right)
\ge
\frac1{1+\lambda}-\alpha .
\]

It remains only to absorb the factor \((1-\alpha)^2\).  Put
\(s=1/(1+\lambda)\).  From \(\lambda>0\), \(0\le s\le1\), and from
\(0\le\alpha\le1\).  Expanding,
\[
2(1-\alpha)^2(s-\alpha)-(2s-6\alpha)
=\alpha\bigl(4-4s+2\alpha s+4\alpha-2\alpha^2\bigr)\ge0.
\]
Hence
\[
2(1-\alpha)^2\left(\frac1{1+\lambda}-\alpha\right)
\ge
\frac2{1+\lambda}-6\alpha .
\]
Combining this inequality with the lower bound on \(J\) proves the claimed
uniform margin.
\end{proof}
