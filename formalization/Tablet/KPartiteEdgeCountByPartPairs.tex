\begin{helper}
Let $G$ be a finite simple $k$-partite graph with part map
$\operatorname{part}:V(G)\to [k]$, so every edge has endpoints in two
different parts.  For $i<j$, let $m_{ij}$ be the number of ordered adjacent
pairs $(u,v)$ with $\operatorname{part}(u)=i$ and
$\operatorname{part}(v)=j$.  Then
\[
|E(G)|=\sum_{i<j} m_{ij}.
\]
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Let $D$ be the finite set of ordered adjacent pairs $(u,v)$ of vertices of
$G$.  The handshaking identity for simple graphs gives
$|D|=2|E(G)|$, because each unordered edge contributes exactly the two
ordered pairs obtained by choosing one of its endpoints first.

Partition $D$ by the ordered pair of parts
$(\operatorname{part}(u),\operatorname{part}(v))$.  For an ordered part-pair
$(a,b)$, let $f(a,b)$ be the size of the corresponding fiber.  Since every
edge goes between two different parts, $f(i,i)=0$ for every $i$.  Also
$f(i,j)=f(j,i)$: the map $(u,v)\mapsto (v,u)$ is a bijection from the fiber
over $(i,j)$ to the fiber over $(j,i)$, using symmetry of adjacency.

Therefore
\[
|D|=\sum_{a,b} f(a,b)
   =\sum_{i<j} f(i,j)+\sum_{j<i} f(i,j)+\sum_i f(i,i)
   =2\sum_{i<j} f(i,j).
\]
For $i<j$, the number $f(i,j)$ is exactly $m_{ij}$.  Combining this identity
with $|D|=2|E(G)|$ and cancelling the factor $2$ gives
$|E(G)|=\sum_{i<j}m_{ij}$.
\end{proof}
