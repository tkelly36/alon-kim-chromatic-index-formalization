\begin{lemma}
Let \(G\) and \(G'\) be two finite graphs sampled by
\(\noderef{RandomIndependentSetSampling}\) with the same parameters
\(\Delta,\gamma\), and put \(p=\gamma/\Delta\).  Assume \(0\le p\le1\).
Then one may choose the activation--priority laws \(\nu,\nu'\) supplied by
the two sampling hypotheses so that they support the matched finite
coordinate-extension mass theorem of
\(\noderef{SamplingFiniteProductMatchedCoordinateExtensionMass}\).

Concretely, for any disjoint source base and extension coordinate sets
\(C,D\), any disjoint target base and extension coordinate sets \(C',D'\),
any compatible prescribed activation patterns, lower cuts, upper cuts, and
thresholds on those coordinates, if the source and target base cells have
the same normalized real mass, if the finite Bernoulli activation factors on
\(D\) and \(D'\) agree, and if the finite priority cut products on the
extension cuts agree, then the source and target extended cylinder masses are
represented by nonnegative real numbers \(m^-,m^+\) with \(m^-=m^+\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Unpack the two \(\noderef{RandomIndependentSetSampling}\) hypotheses and
choose their underlying activation--priority laws \(\nu\) and \(\nu'\).
The definition of \(\noderef{RandomIndependentSetSampling}\) states, for
each chosen law, the total mass identity, the Bernoulli activation-atom
formula, and the full finite priority lower-rectangle formula.  These are
exactly the measure-law inputs required by
\(\noderef{SamplingFiniteProductMatchedCoordinateExtensionMass}\), with the
common parameter \(p=\gamma/\Delta\).

Now fix arbitrary source and target coordinate-extension data satisfying the
displayed hypotheses in the statement.  The assumptions \(0\le p\le1\),
disjointness of base and extension coordinates, containment of the prescribed
activation and priority cuts, disjointness of lower and upper cut families,
and the threshold bounds in \([0,1]\) give the side-specific hypotheses of
\(\noderef{SamplingFiniteProductMatchedCoordinateExtensionMass}\).  The
base-mass compatibility, equality of Bernoulli activation factors, and
equality of extension priority products are exactly the three cross-side
compatibility hypotheses of that node.

Applying \(\noderef{SamplingFiniteProductMatchedCoordinateExtensionMass}\)
therefore produces nonnegative real source and target extension masses,
identifies them with the two one-sided extended cylinders, and proves their
equality.  This is precisely the claimed sampling-law-level matched
coordinate-extension export.
\end{proof}
