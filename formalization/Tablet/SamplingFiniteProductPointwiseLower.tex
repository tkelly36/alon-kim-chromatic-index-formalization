\begin{helper}
Let \(0<1-\alpha\), let \(0\le t\le L<\Delta\), and let
\(N\le\Delta\).  If the fixed-window error bound
\[
\frac{L^2}{\Delta-L}\le -\log(1-\alpha)
\]
holds, then
\[
\left(1-\frac t\Delta\right)^N
\ge
(1-\alpha)\exp\left(-\frac N\Delta t\right).
\]
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Put \(d=\Delta\) and \(u=t/d\).  Since \(0\le t\le L<d\), we have
\(0\le u<1\).  The elementary logarithmic inequality
\[
1-x^{-1}\le \log x\qquad (x>0)
\]
applied to \(x=1-u\) gives
\[
\log(1-u)\ge -\frac{u}{1-u}
= -u-\frac{u^2}{1-u}.
\]
Multiplying by the nonnegative number \(N\), we obtain
\[
N\log(1-u)\ge
-Nu-\frac{Nu^2}{1-u}.
\]

It remains to check that the second-order loss is absorbed by the assumed
window error.  Since \(N\le d\), \(0\le t\le L\), and \(L<d\),
\[
\frac{Nu^2}{1-u}
=\frac{N(t/d)^2}{1-t/d}
\le \frac{t^2}{d-t}
\le \frac{L^2}{d-L}
\le -\log(1-\alpha).
\]
Therefore
\[
\log(1-\alpha)-Nu
\le
-Nu-\frac{Nu^2}{1-u}
\le N\log(1-u).
\]
The left side is
\[
\log\left((1-\alpha)\exp(-Nu)\right),
\]
because \(1-\alpha>0\).  Exponentiating preserves the inequality and yields
\[
(1-\alpha)e^{-Nu}\le (1-u)^N.
\]
Substituting back \(u=t/\Delta\) proves the claimed pointwise finite-product
lower bound.
\end{proof}
