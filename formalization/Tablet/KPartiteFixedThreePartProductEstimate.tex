\begin{helper}
Let $G$ be a finite simple graph and let $A,B,C$ be finite sets of vertices.
Let
\[
T=\#\{(a,b,c)\in A\times B\times C: ab,ac,bc\in E(G)\},
\]
and let
\[
x=\#\{(a,b)\in A\times B: ab\in E(G)\},\quad
y=\#\{(a,c)\in A\times C: ac\in E(G)\},\quad
z=\#\{(b,c)\in B\times C: bc\in E(G)\}.
\]
Then
\[
T\le \sqrt{xyz}.
\]
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
For each $b\in B$, let $T_b$ be the set of triples counted by $T$ whose
middle coordinate is $b$.  Since every counted triple has its middle
coordinate in $B$, the sets $T_b$ partition the set counted by $T$, and hence
\[
T=\sum_{b\in B}|T_b|.
\]
Let $a_b$ be the number of edges from $A$ to $b$, and let $c_b$ be the number
of edges from $b$ to $C$.  Projection of a triple $(a,b,c)\in T_b$ to
$(a,b)$ and $(b,c)$ is injective into the product of these two edge sets, so
\[
|T_b|\le a_b c_b.
\]
Projection of the same triple to $(a,c)$ is injective into the set of
$A$--$C$ edges, because $b$ is fixed inside $T_b$.  Therefore
\[
|T_b|\le y.
\]
Combining the two inequalities gives
\[
|T_b|^2\le a_b c_b y,
\]
and since $|T_b|\ge 0$, this implies
\[
|T_b|\le \sqrt{a_b c_b y}.
\]
Summing over $b\in B$,
\[
T\le \sum_{b\in B}\sqrt{a_b c_b y}
=\sqrt y\sum_{b\in B}\sqrt{a_b}\sqrt{c_b}.
\]
By Cauchy--Schwarz for the finite sum over $B$,
\[
\sum_{b\in B}\sqrt{a_b}\sqrt{c_b}
\le
\sqrt{\sum_{b\in B}a_b}\sqrt{\sum_{b\in B}c_b}.
\]
Finally, double-counting the two endpoint projections gives
\[
\sum_{b\in B}a_b=x,\qquad \sum_{b\in B}c_b=z.
\]
Substituting these identities yields
\[
T\le \sqrt y\,\sqrt x\,\sqrt z=\sqrt{xyz},
\]
as required.
\end{proof}
