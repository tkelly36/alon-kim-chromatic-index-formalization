\begin{helper}
Let $k\in\mathbb N$ with $k\ge2$, and let $x_1,\ldots,x_k$ be real
numbers.  Then
\[
\sum_{1\le i<i'\le k}x_ix_{i'}
\le {k-1\over 2k}\left(\sum_{i=1}^k x_i\right)^2 .
\]
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Put $s=\sum_i x_i$.  Expanding the square of the sum gives
\[
s^2=\sum_i x_i^2+2\sum_{i<i'}x_ix_{i'} .
\]
By Cauchy's inequality,
\[
s^2=\left(\sum_i x_i\cdot 1\right)^2
\le \left(\sum_i x_i^2\right)\left(\sum_i 1^2\right)
=k\sum_i x_i^2 .
\]
Let $P=\sum_{i<i'}x_ix_{i'}$ and $Q=\sum_i x_i^2$.  The first identity says
$2P+Q=s^2$, and the Cauchy inequality says $s^2\le kQ$.  Hence
\[
2kP=k(2P)\le k(2P+Q)-s^2=(k-1)s^2 .
\]
Since $k\ge2$, the positive number $2k$ may be divided out, giving
\[
P\le {k-1\over 2k}s^2 .
\]
\end{proof}
