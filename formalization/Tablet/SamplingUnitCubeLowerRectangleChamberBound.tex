\begin{helper}
Let \(V\) be finite, let \(\nu\) be a finite-on-the-fiber measure on a space
\(\Omega\), let \(F\subseteq\Omega\), and let \(p:\Omega\to\mathbb R^V\).
Assume that on the fiber \(F\) the priority coordinates satisfy the
lower-rectangle law: for every \(t=(t_v)_{v\in V}\in[0,1]^V\),
\[
\nu\{\omega:F(\omega),\ 0\le p(\omega)_v\le t_v\text{ for every }v\}
=M\operatorname{ofReal}\Bigl(\prod_{v\in V}t_v\Bigr),
\]
where \(M=\nu\{\omega:F(\omega)\}\) and this fiber mass is finite.  Let
\(a,b,c\in V\) be pairwise distinct, and let \(S_x,S_y,S_z\subseteq V\) be
pairwise disjoint and disjoint from \(\{a,b,c\}\).  Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\nu\bigl(\{\omega:F(\omega),\ p_a(\omega)\le p_b(\omega)\le p_c(\omega),\\
&\qquad\qquad
  p_w(\omega)<p_a(\omega)\ (w\in S_x),\
  p_w(\omega)<p_b(\omega)\ (w\in S_y),\
  p_w(\omega)<p_c(\omega)\ (w\in S_z)\}\\
&\qquad\qquad\cap\{\omega:p_v(\omega)\in[0,1]\text{ for every }v\in V\}\bigr)
\end{aligned}
\]
is at most
\[
M\operatorname{ofReal}\left(
\int_0^1\int_0^z\int_0^y
x^{|S_x|}y^{|S_y|}z^{|S_z|}\,dx\,dy\,dz\right).
\]
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Restrict \(\nu\) to the fiber \(F\) and push it forward by \(p\) to a finite
measure on \(\mathbb R^V\).  The lower-rectangle hypothesis says that, on each
anchored box \(\prod_v[0,t_v]\) with \(0\le t_v\le1\), this pushforward equals
\(M\) times product Lebesgue measure on the unit cube.  The anchored boxes form
a pi-system generating the Borel sigma-algebra of the finite cube, and the two
finite measures have the same total mass \(M\).  Finite-measure uniqueness on
the generated sigma-algebra therefore identifies the pushed-forward fiber
measure with \(M\) times product Lebesgue measure on every Borel subset of
\([0,1]^V\).

The displayed chamber is Borel, since it is cut out inside the finite cube by
finitely many coordinate inequalities.  Under product Lebesgue measure, fix
\(p_c=z\), \(p_b=y\), and \(p_a=x\) with \(0\le x\le y\le z\le1\).  The sets
\(S_x,S_y,S_z\) are pairwise disjoint and avoid \(a,b,c\), so Fubini separates
the outside-coordinate requirements: the coordinates in \(S_x\) contribute
\(x^{|S_x|}\), those in \(S_y\) contribute \(y^{|S_y|}\), those in \(S_z\)
contribute \(z^{|S_z|}\), and all remaining coordinates contribute \(1\).
The difference between strict and weak outside inequalities lies on finitely
many coordinate hyperplanes, each of product measure zero.  Thus the product
measure of the chamber is precisely the stated iterated integral, and
multiplication by the fiber mass \(M\) gives the claimed bound.
\end{proof}
