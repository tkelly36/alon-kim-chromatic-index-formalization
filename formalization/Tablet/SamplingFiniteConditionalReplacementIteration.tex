\begin{helper}
Let \(\Omega\) be a finite set, let \(n\) be a nonnegative integer, let
\(w:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}\) be a nonnegative weight function, and let
\(P:\mathbb{N}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}\) be a real-valued function.  Suppose
that for every integer \(k<n\) and every \(\omega\in\Omega\),
\[
P(k,\omega)\le P(k+1,\omega).
\]
Then
\[
\sum_{\omega\in\Omega} w(\omega)P(0,\omega)
\le
\sum_{\omega\in\Omega} w(\omega)P(n,\omega).
\]
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
We prove the stronger assertion that for every \(m\le n\),
\[
\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}w(\omega)P(0,\omega)
\le
\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}w(\omega)P(m,\omega).
\]
The case \(m=0\) is equality.  For the induction step, assume the assertion
has been proved for \(m\), and suppose \(m+1\le n\).  Then \(m<n\), so the
hypothesis gives \(P(m,\omega)\le P(m+1,\omega)\) for every
\(\omega\in\Omega\).  Since \(w(\omega)\ge 0\), multiplication by
\(w(\omega)\) preserves the inequality:
\[
w(\omega)P(m,\omega)\le w(\omega)P(m+1,\omega).
\]
Summing over the finite set \(\Omega\) gives
\[
\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}w(\omega)P(m,\omega)
\le
\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}w(\omega)P(m+1,\omega).
\]
Combining this with the induction hypothesis proves the assertion for
\(m+1\).  Taking \(m=n\) gives the displayed finite-iteration inequality.
\end{proof}
