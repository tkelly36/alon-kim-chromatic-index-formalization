\begin{lemma}
Let $k,n\in\mathbb N$ with $k\ge2$, let $C,T>0$, and let
$A=(a_{i,j})_{i\in[k],j\in[n]}$ be a real $k\times n$ table with
$a_{i,j}\ge0$ for every $i,j$.  Suppose that every column has sum at most
$C$,
\[
\sum_{i=1}^k a_{i,j}\le C\qquad\text{for every }j\in[n],
\]
and that the total table sum is at most $T$,
\[
\sum_{i=1}^k\sum_{j=1}^n a_{i,j}\le T.
\]
Then
\[
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{1\le i<i'\le k}a_{i,j}a_{i',j}
\le {k-1\over 2k}\,TC .
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a fixed column $j$, put
\[
s_j=\sum_{i=1}^k a_{i,j}.
\]
The entries in this column are nonnegative, so $s_j\ge0$.  The column-sum
hypothesis gives $s_j\le C$.  Hence
\[
s_j^2\le Cs_j,
\]
because multiplying the inequality $s_j\le C$ by the nonnegative number
$s_j$ preserves the inequality.

We next bound the pair products inside this one column.  For real numbers
$x_1,\ldots,x_k$ with sum $s$, the identity
\[
\sum_{1\le i<i'\le k}x_ix_{i'}
= {k-1\over 2k}s^2
- {1\over 2k}\sum_{1\le i<i'\le k}(x_i-x_{i'})^2
\]
holds by expanding both sides.  Applying this identity to
$x_i=a_{i,j}$ and using that every square is nonnegative gives
\[
\sum_{1\le i<i'\le k}a_{i,j}a_{i',j}
\le {k-1\over 2k}s_j^2
\le {k-1\over 2k}Cs_j .
\]

Summing the last inequality over all columns yields
\[
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{1\le i<i'\le k}a_{i,j}a_{i',j}
\le {k-1\over 2k}C\sum_{j=1}^n s_j.
\]
Finally,
\[
\sum_{j=1}^n s_j
=\sum_{j=1}^n\sum_{i=1}^k a_{i,j}
=\sum_{i=1}^k\sum_{j=1}^n a_{i,j}
\le T
\]
by rearranging the finite double sum and then applying the total-sum
hypothesis.  Since $C>0$, multiplying by
$\frac{k-1}{2k}C\ge0$ preserves the inequality, and therefore
\[
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{1\le i<i'\le k}a_{i,j}a_{i',j}
\le {k-1\over 2k}CT
={k-1\over 2k}TC .
\]
This is the desired bound.
\end{proof}
