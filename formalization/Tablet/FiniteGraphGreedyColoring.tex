\begin{lemma}
Let $G$ be a finite simple graph and $q$ a positive natural number.
If every vertex has degree less than $q$, then there exists a coloring
$c:V(G)\to\{0,\ldots,q-1\}$ with $c(e)\ne c(f)$ whenever $e,f$ are adjacent.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Induct on a finite set $S$ of vertices to color $G[S]$ with the given palette.
The empty set has a coloring. To insert $a\notin S$, collect the colors
of its neighbors in $S$. There are at most $\deg_G(a)<q$ such colors,
so some palette color is absent. Assign it to $a$ and keep the colors on
$S$. Edges inside $S$ remain properly colored, and every edge from $a$
has different colors at its ends. Applying this induction to all vertices
proves the claim.
\end{proof}
