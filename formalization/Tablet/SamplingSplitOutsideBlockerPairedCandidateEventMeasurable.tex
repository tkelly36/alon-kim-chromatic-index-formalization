\begin{helper}
Every paired candidate event \(F_{P,T}(x)\) defined in
\noderef{SamplingSplitOutsideBlockerPairedCandidateEvent} is measurable
in the product of the two activation--priority spaces, with the full
sigma algebras on finite activation sets and Borel sigma algebras on
the real priority coordinates. No graph or coordinate injectivity
assumptions are needed for this measurability assertion.
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Expand \noderef{SamplingSplitOutsideBlockerPairedCandidateEvent}.
Activation membership at any fixed vertex is the preimage of a subset
of the finite activation space under the corresponding coordinate
projection, hence measurable. Each priority evaluation is a measurable
coordinate map. A strict comparison between two evaluations is measurable
as the preimage of the open set \(\{(a,b):a<b\}\); a test that an
evaluation belongs to \([0,1]\) is measurable as the preimage of a closed
interval. The negation of a blocker conjunction is consequently measurable.
The embedded-neighbor clauses are finite intersections of implications,
and an implication is a union of the complement of its antecedent with its
consequent. All graph and index membership antecedents are constant events.
The private blocker clauses also quantify over proofs of a fixed
proposition. If no such proof exists this clause is the whole state space;
otherwise proof irrelevance identifies all its values, leaving the single
measurable blocker test. Finally intersect the two activation conditions,
the embedded-neighbor clauses, and the finitely many private and retained
tests. This is precisely \(F_{P,T}(x)\).
\end{proof}
