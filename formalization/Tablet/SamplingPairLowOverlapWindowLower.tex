\begin{helper}
Let \(\Delta>0\), let \(0\le\gamma_0\le\gamma\le\Delta\), and let
\(\gamma_0<\Delta\).  Fix a natural exponent \(N\) with
\(N\le\Delta\), and suppose \(N/\Delta=\lambda\).  Assume the compact-window
error condition
\[
\frac{\gamma_0^2}{\Delta-\gamma_0}\le -\log(1-\alpha)
\]
with \(1-\alpha>0\).  If the exponential low-overlap window has the margin
\[
2\int_0^{\gamma_0}\int_x^{\gamma_0}
(1-\alpha)e^{-\lambda x}(1-\alpha)e^{-y}\,dy\,dx
\ge
\frac{2}{1+\lambda}-6\alpha,
\]
then the full finite pair chamber satisfies
\[
\frac2{\Delta^2}\int_0^\gamma\int_x^\gamma
\left(1-\frac{x}{\Delta}\right)^N
\left(1-\frac{y}{\Delta}\right)^\Delta\,dy\,dx
\ge
\frac{2/(1+\lambda)-6\alpha}{\Delta^2}.
\]
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
On the fixed window \(0\le x\le y\le\gamma_0\), the hypotheses
\(\gamma_0<\Delta\), \(N\le\Delta\), and
\(\gamma_0^2/(\Delta-\gamma_0)\le-\log(1-\alpha)\) allow us to apply
\noderef{SamplingFiniteProductPointwiseLower}.  For the first factor, using
\(N/\Delta=\lambda\), this gives
\[
\left(1-\frac{x}{\Delta}\right)^N
\ge (1-\alpha)e^{-\lambda x}.
\]
For the second factor, applying the same node with exponent \(\Delta\) gives
\[
\left(1-\frac{y}{\Delta}\right)^\Delta
\ge (1-\alpha)e^{-y}.
\]
Since \(1-\alpha>0\), both exponential lower factors are nonnegative.
Multiplying the two pointwise inequalities therefore yields, throughout the
window,
\[
\left(1-\frac{x}{\Delta}\right)^N
\left(1-\frac{y}{\Delta}\right)^\Delta
\ge
(1-\alpha)e^{-\lambda x}(1-\alpha)e^{-y}.
\]

Both sides are continuous functions on the compact chamber.  Interval-integral
monotonicity in \(y\in[x,\gamma_0]\) and then in \(x\in[0,\gamma_0]\) gives
\[
\int_0^{\gamma_0}\int_x^{\gamma_0}
(1-\alpha)e^{-\lambda x}(1-\alpha)e^{-y}\,dy\,dx
\le
\int_0^{\gamma_0}\int_x^{\gamma_0}
\left(1-\frac{x}{\Delta}\right)^N
\left(1-\frac{y}{\Delta}\right)^\Delta\,dy\,dx .
\]
The finite integrand is nonnegative on the relevant chamber, so enlarging
first the inner interval from \([x,\gamma_0]\) to \([x,\gamma]\), and then
the outer interval from \([0,\gamma_0]\) to \([0,\gamma]\), can only increase
the integral.  Hence the exponential window integral is at most the full
finite chamber integral over \(0\le x\le y\le\gamma\).

Multiplying this comparison by the positive scale \(2/\Delta^2\) and using
the assumed exponential-window margin gives
\[
\frac2{\Delta^2}\int_0^\gamma\int_x^\gamma
\left(1-\frac{x}{\Delta}\right)^N
\left(1-\frac{y}{\Delta}\right)^\Delta\,dy\,dx
\ge
\frac{2/(1+\lambda)-6\alpha}{\Delta^2},
\]
as claimed.
\end{proof}
