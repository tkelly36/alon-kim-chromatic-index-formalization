\begin{lemma}
Let $k$ be a positive integer.  If $G$ is a $k$-partite graph with $m$ edges,
then the number of triangles in $G$ is at most
\[
\binom{k}{3}\left(\frac{m}{\binom{k}{2}}\right)^{3/2}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a $k$-partition $V(G)=A_1\cup\cdots\cup A_k$.  For
$1\le i<j\le k$, write $m_{ij}$ for the number of edges of $G$ between
$A_i$ and $A_j$.  Since $G$ is $k$-partite, every edge has its endpoints in
two distinct parts, so
\[
\sum_{1\le i<j\le k}m_{ij}=m. \tag{1}
\]
Also every triangle has its three vertices in three distinct partite
classes.  Thus the total triangle count is the sum, over triples
$i<j<\ell$, of the number of triangles using one vertex in each of
$A_i,A_j,A_\ell$.

If $k=1$ or $k=2$, there are no triples of distinct partite classes.  Hence
there are no triangles in $G$.  The factor $\binom{k}{3}$ is zero in these
two cases.  Thus the right side of the formal Lean statement is zero by its
leading factor, and the asserted inequality is the trivial inequality
$0\le 0$; this case analysis does not use the displayed quotient by
$\binom{k}{2}$.  We may therefore assume $k\ge 3$ for the rest of the proof.

For a fixed triple $i<j<\ell$, consider the induced tripartite graph on
$A_i\cup A_j\cup A_\ell$.  Its three pairwise edge counts are
$m_{ij},m_{i\ell},m_{j\ell}$.  We prove directly that the number of triangles
in this three-part subgraph is at most
\[
\sqrt{m_{ij}m_{i\ell}m_{j\ell}}.
\]
Write the three parts temporarily as $A,B,C$, and write their pairwise edge
counts as $x=e(A,B)$, $y=e(A,C)$, and $z=e(B,C)$.  For $b\in B$, let $a_b$
be the number of neighbors of $b$ in $A$, let $c_b$ be the number of
neighbors of $b$ in $C$, and let $t_b$ be the number of triangles whose
$B$-vertex is $b$.  Such a triangle is an $A$-$C$ edge with one endpoint in
$N_A(b)$ and the other in $N_C(b)$, so $t_b\le a_bc_b$.  Also $t_b\le y$,
because there are only $y$ total $A$-$C$ edges.  Thus
$t_b^2\le a_bc_by$, and so $t_b\le \sqrt{a_bc_by}$.  Summing over $b$ and
using Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
T=\sum_{b\in B}t_b
\le \sqrt y\sum_{b\in B}\sqrt{a_bc_b}
\le \sqrt y\sqrt{\sum_b a_b}\sqrt{\sum_b c_b}
=\sqrt{xyz}.
\]
Here $\sum_b a_b=x$ and $\sum_b c_b=z$ by double-counting the $A$-$B$ and
$B$-$C$ edges.  Restoring the notation for the fixed triple
$i,j,\ell$ gives the displayed product estimate.

Summing this product estimate over all triples of parts gives
\[
T(G)\le
\sum_{1\le i<j<\ell\le k}\sqrt{m_{ij}m_{i\ell}m_{j\ell}}. \tag{2}
\]

\noderef{KPartiteWeightedTriangleInequality} applied to the nonnegative
weights $w_{ij}=m_{ij}$ now gives
\[
\sum_{i<j<\ell}\sqrt{m_{ij}m_{i\ell}m_{j\ell}}
\le
\binom{k}{3}
\left(\frac{m}{\binom{k}{2}}\right)^{3/2}.
\]
The total weight in that node is $m$ by (1).  Combining this estimate with
(2) proves the asserted triangle bound.
\end{proof}
