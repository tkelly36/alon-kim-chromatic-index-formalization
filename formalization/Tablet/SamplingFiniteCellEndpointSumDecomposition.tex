\begin{helper}
Let \((S,\mathcal A)\) be a measurable space, let \(\nu\) be a measure on
this measurable space, let \(E\subseteq S\) be a measurable event, and let
\(U\subseteq S\) be a support for \(E\), in the sense that
\(\nu(E\setminus U)=0\).  Let
\(\{C_\omega:\omega\in\Omega\}\) be a finite family of pairwise disjoint
measurable cells contained in \(U\) whose intersections with \(E\) cover the
supported part of \(E\):
\[
E\cap U=\bigsqcup_{\omega\in\Omega}(E\cap C_\omega).
\]
Suppose that for each \(\omega\) there are real numbers \(w_\omega\ge0\) and
\(P_\omega\ge0\), with the zero-mass convention \(P_\omega=0\) whenever
\(w_\omega=0\), such that
\[
\operatorname{ofReal}(w_\omega P_\omega)=\nu(E\cap C_\omega).
\]
Then the event mass is the finite weighted sum
\[
\operatorname{ofReal}\!\left(\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}w_\omega P_\omega\right)
=\nu(E).
\]
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Since the cells \(C_\omega\) are pairwise disjoint, the sets
\(E\cap C_\omega\) are also pairwise disjoint.  Since each cell is contained
in \(U\), the displayed cover says that their finite union is exactly
\(E\cap U\).  Finite additivity of the measure gives
\[
\nu(E\cap U)=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}\nu(E\cap C_\omega)
\]
as a finite sum in the extended nonnegative reals.

For each \(\omega\), the assumed cell identity rewrites the corresponding
summand as \(\operatorname{ofReal}(w_\omega P_\omega)\).  The factors are
nonnegative: \(w_\omega\ge0\) and \(P_\omega\ge0\), while the zero-mass
convention merely fixes the otherwise arbitrary normalized probability on
null cells and ensures that a null cell contributes the real number \(0\).
Thus every real summand \(w_\omega P_\omega\) is nonnegative.  For a finite
sum of nonnegative real numbers, \(\operatorname{ofReal}\) commutes with the
sum.  Therefore
\[
\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}\operatorname{ofReal}(w_\omega P_\omega)
=
\operatorname{ofReal}\!\left(\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}w_\omega P_\omega\right).
\]
Finally \(E\) is the disjoint union of \(E\cap U\) and \(E\setminus U\), and
the latter has measure \(0\) by hypothesis.  Thus \(\nu(E)=\nu(E\cap U)\).
Combining this equality with finite additivity proves the claimed endpoint
sum formula.
\end{proof}
