\begin{helper}
Let \(V\) be a finite vertex set and let \(a\neq b\).  Let \(S,T\subseteq V\)
be disjoint from \(\{a,b\}\), and assume also that \(S\cap T=\varnothing\).
For \(0\le y\le x\le 1\), consider the fiber of the product cube in which
the two distinguished priority coordinates have values \(q(a)=x\) and
\(q(b)=y\).  In that fiber, the set of remaining coordinates for which
\(q(w)<q(a)\) for every \(w\in S\), \(q(w)<q(b)\) for every \(w\in T\), and
all coordinates lie in \([0,1]\), has Lebesgue volume at most
\[
x^{|S|}y^{|T|}.
\]
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Write
\[
\beta=\{v\in V:v\neq a,\ v\neq b\}
\]
for the remaining coordinate set.  On \(\beta\) define an upper-bound vector
\[
U(r)=
\begin{cases}
x,& r\in S,\\
y,& r\in T,\\
1,& r\notin S\cup T.
\end{cases}
\]
This is well-defined because \(S\) and \(T\) are disjoint.  Any point in the
fiber described in the statement has every remaining coordinate nonnegative;
if the coordinate lies in \(S\) it is \(<q(a)=x\), if it lies in \(T\) it is
\(<q(b)=y\), and otherwise the cube condition gives it at most \(1\).  Hence
the fiber set is contained in the rectangle \(\prod_{r\in\beta}[0,U(r)]\).

The volume of this rectangle is the product of the side lengths:
\[
\prod_{r\in\beta} U(r).
\]
The side lengths are \(x\) on the coordinates of \(S\), \(y\) on the
coordinates of \(T\), and \(1\) on all other remaining coordinates.  Since
\(S\) and \(T\) are disjoint and neither contains \(a\) or \(b\), the product
is exactly \(x^{|S|}y^{|T|}\).  Monotonicity of Lebesgue measure under
containment gives the asserted upper bound for the fiber volume.
\end{proof}
