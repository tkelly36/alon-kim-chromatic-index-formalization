\begin{helper}
Let $\Delta>0$, let $0\le\gamma\le\Delta$, let $a>0$ and $\rho\ge0$, and
let $N,L$ be natural-number exponents satisfying
\[
\frac N\Delta=a,\qquad \frac L\Delta=\rho .
\]
Then the single paired full-graph chamber integral satisfies
\[
2\int_{0\le u\le v\le w\le\gamma}
\left(1-\frac w\Delta\right)^\Delta
\left(1-\frac v\Delta\right)^N
\left(1-\frac u\Delta\right)^L\,dw\,dv\,du
\le
\frac{2}{(1+a)(1+a+\rho)}.
\]
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Fix a point in the chamber $0\le u\le v\le w\le\gamma$.  Since
$\gamma\le\Delta$ and $\Delta>0$, we have
$0\le u/\Delta\le v/\Delta\le w/\Delta\le1$.  Thus the three bases
$1-u/\Delta$, $1-v/\Delta$, and $1-w/\Delta$ are all nonnegative and at most
$1$.

For every $t\in[0,1]$ the elementary exponential inequality
$1-t\le e^{-t}$ holds.  Applying this to $w/\Delta$, $v/\Delta$, and
$u/\Delta$, and using monotonicity of powers with nonnegative bases, gives
\[
\left(1-\frac w\Delta\right)^\Delta
\left(1-\frac v\Delta\right)^N
\left(1-\frac u\Delta\right)^L
\le
e^{-w}e^{-av}e^{-\rho u}.
\]
Indeed,
$(e^{-w/\Delta})^\Delta=e^{-w}$, while
$(e^{-v/\Delta})^N=e^{-(N/\Delta)v}=e^{-av}$ and
$(e^{-u/\Delta})^L=e^{-(L/\Delta)u}=e^{-\rho u}$ by the two ratio
hypotheses.

The exponential kernel is nonnegative everywhere.  Therefore enlarging the
domain from the finite chamber $0\le u\le v\le w\le\gamma$ to the infinite
ordered chamber $0\le u\le v\le w<\infty$ can only increase the integral.
Consequently the left-hand side is at most
\[
2\int_0^\infty\int_u^\infty\int_v^\infty
e^{-w}e^{-av}e^{-\rho u}\,dw\,dv\,du .
\]

The integral is evaluated successively.  First
\[
\int_v^\infty e^{-w}\,dw=e^{-v}.
\]
Then, because $a>0$ and hence $1+a>0$,
\[
\int_u^\infty e^{-(1+a)v}\,dv
=\frac{e^{-(1+a)u}}{1+a}.
\]
Finally, since $\rho\ge0$, we have $1+a+\rho>0$, and so
\[
\int_0^\infty \frac{e^{-(1+a+\rho)u}}{1+a}\,du
=\frac{1}{(1+a)(1+a+\rho)}.
\]
Multiplying by the leading factor $2$ gives exactly
\[
2\int_{0\le u\le v\le w\le\gamma}
\left(1-\frac w\Delta\right)^\Delta
\left(1-\frac v\Delta\right)^N
\left(1-\frac u\Delta\right)^L\,dw\,dv\,du
\le
\frac{2}{(1+a)(1+a+\rho)}.
\]
\end{proof}
