\begin{lemma}
A tripartite graph $G$ has at most
\[
\frac{1}{12}\sum_{v\in V(G)}\deg_G(v)^2
\]
triangles.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let the three vertex classes be $A_1,A_2,A_3$, and let $T_v$ be the number of
triangles containing a fixed vertex $v$.  Every triangle has exactly three
vertices, so
\[
3T=\sum_{v\in V(G)}T_v .
\]
If $v\in A_i$, then every triangle through $v$ chooses one neighbor of $v$ in
each of the other two classes.  Thus, writing those neighbor counts as
$p$ and $q$, we have $T_v\le pq$.  Since $p+q=\deg_G(v)$ and
$pq\le ((p+q)/2)^2$, this gives
\[
T_v\le \left(\frac{\deg_G(v)}2\right)^2 .
\]
Summing over $v$ and dividing by $3$ gives
\[
T\le \frac13\sum_{v\in V(G)}\frac{\deg_G(v)^2}{4}
=\frac1{12}\sum_{v\in V(G)}\deg_G(v)^2,
\]
as claimed.
\end{proof}
